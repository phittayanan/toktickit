# Lab 3 Grilling Decisions

The full record behind Selected Key Prompt 3 of [`ai-use.md`](ai-use.md).

After I invoked the grilling skill, the orchestrating agent asked the open
design questions of Lab 3 one at a time, each with lettered options and a
recommendation. I answered each with one letter. The eleven answers were
then confirmed together and became the acceptance criteria that the
specification agent wrote [`specification.md`](specification.md) from.

How to read the table:

- **What the AI asked** is AI-written: the question's topic and the
  options it offered, shortened from the session.
- **My answer** is my own input, verbatim. Every answer was a single
  letter.
- **What it led to** is an AI-written summary of the settled decision.

Q6 is the one answer that departed from the AI's recommendation.

| # | What the AI asked (AI-written) | My answer | What it led to (AI-written summary) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | How the login is remembered. A: a server-side session stored in PostgreSQL with an httpOnly cookie. B: a signed token (JWT) held by the browser. | `A` | A server-side session in PostgreSQL, with an httpOnly cookie that carries only the session reference. Logout deletes the session, and deactivating a user ends their sessions at once. |
| Q2 | Password rules for a password the user chooses. A: follow the handout mockup (at least 8 characters with upper case, lower case, a digit, and a special character, shown as a live checklist). B: length only (at least 12). | `A` | At least 8 characters with upper case, lower case, a digit, and a special character, shown as a live checklist. |
| Q3 | Repeated failed logins. A: a temporary throttle that clears by itself. B: no limit, a generic error only. | `A` | Five failed attempts for the same email within 15 minutes refuse logins for that email for 15 minutes, then clear by themselves. There is no unlock workflow. |
| Q4 | How far an Administrator may operate on Tickets. A: the same Ticket rights as IT Staff, plus User Management. B: read-only on Tickets. C: no Ticket access. | `A` | An Administrator has every IT Staff Ticket permission and, in addition, the Administrator-only User Management screen. IT Staff cannot reach User Management. |
| Q5 | Who may reassign a Ticket that already has an owner. A: any IT Staff or Administrator. B: only the current owner or an Administrator. | `A` | Any IT Staff or Administrator may claim an unassigned Ticket and may change the owner of any Ticket. Taking a Ticket from another owner needs a confirmation. |
| Q6 | What happens when a Ticket Owner is deactivated or loses a staff role. A: block the change until their Tickets are reassigned. B: return their unfinished Tickets to the queue automatically. C: leave ownership as it is. | `B` | The owner's unfinished Tickets become unassigned in the same operation. Finished Tickets (Closed, Cancelled) keep the historical owner. The confirmation states how many Tickets return to the queue. |
| Q7 | The staff-side status transition matrix. A: the proposed matrix with Closed as a final status. B: the same, but Closed may go to Reopened. | `A` | New to Open or Cancelled; Open to In Progress, Waiting for Requester, or Cancelled; In Progress to Waiting for Requester, Resolved, or Cancelled; Waiting for Requester to In Progress, Resolved, or Cancelled; Resolved to Closed or Reopened; Reopened to In Progress, Waiting for Requester, Resolved, or Cancelled. Closed and Cancelled are final. Claiming a New Ticket moves it to Open. Resolved needs an owner and a Resolution Summary the Requester can see. |
| Q8 | What "Problem Appears Resolved" does. A: a flag on the Ticket, status unchanged. B: a canned Public Comment only. C: both. | `A` | The action sets a timestamped flag on the Ticket and never changes its status. Staff see it as a badge, can filter on it, and alone decide whether to set Resolved. |
| Q9 | Whether a Requester may change the status of their own Ticket. A: no status changes at all. B: may cancel while New and reopen while Resolved. | `A` | A Requester never changes status. Cancelling or reopening is asked for in a Public Comment and done by staff. |
| Q10 | Initial passwords for the Requesters migrated from Lab 2. A: migrate them with no usable password. B: the migration embeds one shared initial password. | `A` | Each migrated Requester becomes a User with no password and cannot log in until an Administrator sets an initial password. The seed gives only the seed accounts a documented local-development initial password that must be changed at first login. No password or hash is embedded in a migration. |
| Q11 | How an Administrator sets an initial password. A: generated by the system and shown once. B: typed by the Administrator. | `A` | On user creation and on "set new initial password" the system generates a random temporary password and shows it once. It cannot be viewed again. |
